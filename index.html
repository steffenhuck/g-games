The Pantheon

Great papers, off the pedestal and into your hands. Each one a game built from what the paper found.

← All games
  • 1 Foot's Bad Man Oxford, 1967. You are the bad man. Three good people, each living by a rule; six traps in your hand, a runaway tram among them. Spring them, say what you would have done, and let the audience judge. Foot's own rule gives you nothing.
  • 2 Einstein's Flower Merchant Bern, 1905. You sell flowers to the stars, by rocket. The faster you fly, the fresher they land and the later you are paid. Die rich.
  • 3 Curie's Counterfactuals Paris, 1898. A spot of light, a quartz, a notebook. Measure what substances do, well or badly. Every number you write grows a world. At the end, see the one you made, a century on.
  • 4 Benjamin's Veil Paris, 1936. One painted face with a halo, six ages, a new way to copy it each age. Show it and spend the halo. Keep it and watch the eyes of the age change.
  • 5 Lovelace's Cards London, 1843. A loom of wheels and a stack of cards that program them. Six orders to begin with. Harder ones optional.
  • 6 Cantor's Bargain Halle, 1891. The Great Lister's endless book has every row of m's and w's, he says. Write one it doesn't. You can't. Then Cantor makes you an offer.
  • 7 Goodall's Long Grass Gombe Stream, 1970. You are a young chimpanzee in the termite season. Pick a stem in the long grass, push it down the hole, draw it out slowly. Goodall's red pencil says how old you fished.
  • 8 Watson's Cardboard Cambridge, 1953. Four bases cut from card, one chain given. Build the other so the rails stay straight. Then see whether it copies.
  • 9 Noether's Ball Göttingen, 1918. A ball, and nine ways to change how it is thrown. Each comes with a formula. Say which changes the formula notices and which it doesn't.
  • 10 Brown's Restless Dust London, 1828. Dust is dancing in a drop of water. Grind a chip of the Sphinx, burn hair in a candle, seal the drop in oil. Seven days to make it lie still.
  • 11 Weil's Vase Marseille, 1940. You are the soul inside a warrior. Twelve days of war, and Zeus's scales say whose day it is. Strike, take, pass or raise the man before you. Every blow, given or taken, turns you a band more to stone.
  • 12 Hubble's Twelve Nights Mount Wilson, 1929. Twelve nights on the 100-inch. Swing the telescope, judge how far, turn the measuring screw, pencil a dot. Then say how fast.
